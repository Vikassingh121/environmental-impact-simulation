📐 Simulation Methodology & Data Sources
  • Oxygen Production: Based on USDA Forest Service estimates — one mature tree produces ~100 kg O₂/year. Dense plantations (Miyawaki method) can have 400-800 trees/acre.
  • CO₂ Absorption: Average mature tree absorbs ~22 kg CO₂/year (EPA estimates). Dense canopy trees absorb more. We use a density-weighted model.
  • Temperature Reduction: Research from IIT Bombay and IISC Bangalore shows dense urban tree cover reduces local temperatures by 2-8°C. We model based on canopy cover ratio within a 2km radius.
  • PM2.5 Filtration: Dense tree canopies trap 7-24% of particulate matter in a 500m radius (Nature Sustainability, 2022). We use a conservative 15% reduction per micro-sanctuary zone.
  • Evapotranspiration Cooling: Each large tree transpires ~400 liters/day in summer, producing cooling equivalent to 2 room ACs (US EPA). Scaled by tree density.
  • Gas Composition: Atmospheric baselines from NOAA. Local deviations modeled using urban pollution gradients and photosynthesis rates per canopy area.
  • Micro-Sanctuary Count: Calculated as city area ÷ (π × 2²) = area ÷ 12.57 km² per sanctuary zone (non-overlapping circles in a 2km radius grid).
  • Note: This is an indicative model. Real-world outcomes depend on tree species, soil, water table, existing infrastructure, and maintenance. Numbers represent scientifically-grounded estimates, not precise predictions.
